\begin{abstract}
Part~I. Colouring the cells of a finite-difference heat simulation by the
parity of $\lfloor q\,u\rfloor$ produces black-and-white frames whose
apparent complexity rises from nothing, peaks in a lattice of symmetric
concentric islands, and dies. We show that these frames are the contour
map of the temperature at spacing $1/q$ with alternating fill, that the
rise is aliasing, and that the islands are the replicated centres of an
undersampled Fresnel zone plate. Three elementary results make this
exact: the band count and the total variation are nonincreasing under
Neumann heat flow on a box; the phase-averaged perimeter of the sampled
pattern equals a sum over grid edges of a triangle wave of the edge
jump, $\sum_e \dist(q|\Delta_e u|,2\Z)$, and the discrete total
variation is nonincreasing, so every increase of the perimeter is
produced by edges whose jump exceeds one quantum; and the block entropy
of the pattern has two non-commuting limits, $0$ as the grid is refined
and maximal as the quantum is refined. Part~II. We then move the
modulus from the display into the dynamics, which yields Wolfram's
continuous cellular automata, and ask how their class-4 localized
structures interact with the $3^n$ boundary strata of a box when the
walls are reflecting, implemented by the ternary-cube rule.
\end{abstract}

\part{Quantized diffusion: what the pictures are}

\section{The observation}\label{sec:obs}

The two-dimensional heat equation $u_t=\kappa\Delta u$ was integrated on
a $64\times64$ grid with an explicit scheme, starting from a hot square
($u=100$ on a $24\times24$ block, $0$ elsewhere), and displayed as a
height field in which each facet is coloured white or black according to
the parity of $\lfloor 10\,u\rfloor$. The frames (Figure~\ref{fig:frames})
begin uniformly black, erupt into a fine noise-like texture around the
square, organize into a square-symmetric lattice of concentric islands,
and finally fade to a few rings and then to a single colour. The
sequence looks like a cellular automaton with a rich transient, and the
islands look like eddies. Neither is the case, and the point of Part~I
is to say precisely what they are.

Throughout, $\Omega=(0,L)^n$, $u$ solves $u_t=\kappa\Delta u$ with
homogeneous Neumann data, $q>0$ is the quantization frequency (quantum
$1/q$), $\theta\in[0,1)$ is a phase, and the \emph{pattern} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pattern}
  b_\theta(x,t)=\lfloor q\,u(x,t)+\theta\rfloor \bmod 2 .
\end{equation}
The grid version samples $u$ at the $N^n$ nodes of the explicit scheme
with reflected ghost values (the ternary-cube rule with zero flux), under
$\sum_m r_m\le\frac12$. We write $E$ for the set of grid edges and
$\Delta_eu$ for the jump of $u$ across the edge $e$.

So \eqref{eq:pattern} is the contour map of $u$ with isotherms at the
levels $(k-\theta)/q$ and alternating fill. The pattern is an
\emph{observable} of $u$, not a state: two fields with the same pattern
evolve to different patterns, so no local rule on black and white cells
reproduces the frames, and by the usual definition (a map on
configurations over a finite alphabet given by a local rule) this is not
a cellular automaton.

\section{What is monotone in the continuum}\label{sec:cont}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:cont}
Let $u$ solve the Neumann heat equation on the box $\Omega$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The band count $B_q(t)=\#\{k:(k-\theta)/q\in[\min u(\cdot,t),\max u(\cdot,t)]\}$ is nonincreasing for every $\theta$.
\item The total variation $\TV(t)=\int_\Omega|\nabla u(x,t)|\,dx$ is nonincreasing.
\item The phase-averaged total isotherm length equals the total variation exactly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coarea}
  \int_0^1\sum_{k\in\Z}\mathcal H^{n-1}\bigl(\{u(\cdot,t)=(k-\theta)/q\}\bigr)\,d\theta
  \;=\; q\,\TV(t),
\end{equation}
and is therefore nonincreasing.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) is the maximum principle: $\max u$ is nonincreasing and $\min u$
nondecreasing. (2) Reflect $u$ evenly across every face of the box; the
result $\tilde u$ is a $2L$-periodic solution of the heat equation on the
torus $\mathbb T=(\R/2L\Z)^n$, smooth for $t>0$. Each component
$\partial_m\tilde u$ is again a periodic heat solution, so
$\nabla\tilde u(t)=K_{t-s}*\nabla\tilde u(s)$ for $t>s$, where $K$ is the
periodic heat kernel, $K\ge0$, $\int K=1$. Convexity of the Euclidean
norm gives $|\nabla\tilde u(t)|\le K_{t-s}*|\nabla\tilde u(s)|$
pointwise; integrating over $\mathbb T$ and dividing by $2^n$ gives
$\TV(t)\le\TV(s)$. (3) By the coarea formula,
$\int_\Omega|\nabla u|=\int_\R\mathcal H^{n-1}(\{u=s\})\,ds$; the left
side of \eqref{eq:coarea} is $\int_\R\mathcal H^{n-1}(\{u=s\})\,q\,ds$
after the substitution $s=(k-\theta)/q$, $\theta\in[0,1)$, $k\in\Z$.
\end{proof}

For a fixed phase the sum in \eqref{eq:coarea} is a Riemann sum of the
coarea integral, hence within $o(1)$ (as $q\to\infty$) of a
nonincreasing function. In the continuum the quantized contour map has
no rise of complexity, for any quantum, in the phase-averaged sense.

\section{The exact perimeter formula on the grid}\label{sec:grid}

Let $P_h$ be the number of grid edges whose two cells differ in colour,
and $C_h=\sum_{e\in E}|\lfloor qu_a+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor qu_b+\theta\rfloor|$
the number of isotherm crossings, where $a,b$ are the ends of $e$. Let
$\TV_h=\sum_{e\in E}|\Delta_eu|$ and let $\varphi(x)=\dist(x,2\Z)$ be the
triangle wave of period $2$ with $\varphi(x)=x$ on $[0,1]$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:grid}
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Discrete TVD.) For each axis $m$, $\sum_{\mathbf i}|u_{\mathbf i+\mathbf e_m}-u_{\mathbf i}|$ is nonincreasing under the explicit scheme with reflected ghosts and $\sum_mr_m\le\frac12$. Hence $\TV_h$ is nonincreasing.
\item (Random phase.) With $\theta$ uniform on $[0,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
  \E_\theta C_h=q\,\TV_h,\qquad
  \E_\theta P_h=\sum_{e\in E}\varphi\bigl(q|\Delta_eu|\bigr),
\end{equation}
both exactly.
\item Consequently: if $q|\Delta_eu|\le1$ on every edge then $\E_\theta P_h=q\TV_h$ and is nonincreasing; always $\E_\theta P_h\le\min(q\TV_h,|E|)$; every increase of $\E_\theta P_h$ in time is produced by edges whose jump exceeds one quantum and is decreasing through a folded branch of $\varphi$; and $\E_\theta P_h$ is nonincreasing from the first time $t^\ast$ at which $\max_e q|\Delta_eu|\le1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) On the reflected periodic grid the scheme is a convex combination of
translates, $u^{l+1}=\sum_jc_j\,\sigma_ju^l$ with $c_j\ge0$,
$\sum c_j=1$, by the CFL condition. The axis difference
$D_mu=\sigma_{\mathbf e_m}u-u$ satisfies the same recursion, and a
convex combination of translates is an $\ell^1$-contraction, so
$\|D_mu^{l+1}\|_1\le\|D_mu^l\|_1$. Restricting the periodic sum to the
box changes it by the factor $2^n$. (2) Under a uniform shift the number
of levels $(k-\theta)/q$ inside an interval of length $\ell$ is
$\lfloor q\ell\rfloor$ with probability $1-\{q\ell\}$ and
$\lfloor q\ell\rfloor+1$ with probability $\{q\ell\}$. Its expectation is
$q\ell$, which gives the first identity. The two ends of the edge differ
in colour iff that number is odd; if $\lfloor q\ell\rfloor$ is even this
has probability $\{q\ell\}$, otherwise $1-\{q\ell\}$, and both equal
$\varphi(q\ell)$. (3) follows from $\varphi(x)=x$ on $[0,1]$,
$\varphi\le\min(x,1)$, and the fact that $\varphi$ is increasing only on
the branches $[2j-1,2j]$, $j\ge1$, which require $x>1$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:grid} explains the frames. Initially the jumps across
the edge of the hot square are a thousand quanta, $\varphi$ is folded a
thousand times, and the expected perimeter per such edge is about
$\frac12$. As the front spreads the number of edges in the folded regime
grows, so the perimeter grows, although $q\TV_h$ is falling all along.
When the largest jump passes below one quantum, the perimeter becomes
$q\TV_h$ itself and decays with it. Table~\ref{tab:phase} shows the
identity \eqref{eq:phase} against a Monte-Carlo average over $200$
phases on the setup of Figure~\ref{fig:frames}, and the peak at the step
where the largest jump crosses one quantum.

\section{Entropy: the limits do not commute}\label{sec:entropy}

Let $H_{q,h}(t)$ be the Shannon entropy of the empirical distribution of
$2\times2$ blocks of $b_\theta$ on the grid of spacing $h$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:entropy}
Fix $t>0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For fixed $q$, $\lim_{h\to0}H_{q,h}(t)=0$.
\item For fixed $h$, if $1,u(x_1,t),\dots,u(x_M,t)$ are linearly independent over $\Q$, where $x_j$ are the grid nodes, then $(q\,u(x_j,t)\bmod2)_{j}$ is equidistributed on $(\R/2\Z)^M$ as $q\to\infty$, and the Ces\`aro limit over $q$ of $H_{q,h}(t)$ is the maximum $\log_216=4$ bits.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) For $t>0$ the isotherms are smooth curves of finite total length
$\Lambda_q(t)$; a $2\times2$ block is non-uniform only if an isotherm
passes within distance $2h$ of it, so the fraction of non-uniform blocks
is $O(h\Lambda_q(t))$, and the entropy of a distribution concentrated on
one symbol with mass $1-O(h)$ is $O(h\log(1/h))$. (2) is Weyl's
equidistribution theorem (Kronecker--Weyl) for the sequence
$q\mapsto(qu(x_j,t))_j$ on the torus, under the stated rational
independence; the uniform distribution on $\{0,1\}^4$ maximizes the
block entropy.
\end{proof}

The square-symmetric initial data of the experiment violate the
independence in (2), since $u(x_j)=u(\sigma x_j)$ for the symmetries
$\sigma$ of the square, and the achievable entropy is reduced by the
orbit structure. That is why the aliased frames look like symmetric
designs rather than noise. Measured on the frames of
Figure~\ref{fig:frames}, the block entropy reaches $3.99$ bits at
step~$400$; this is regime~(2), a statement about the sampling, not
about the heat equation.

\section{The islands are moir\'e}\label{sec:moire}

Where $u$ is locally linear, $\lfloor q(a\cdot x)+\theta\rfloor\bmod2$ on
the lattice is a two-colour digital plane, whose one-dimensional sections
are Sturmian words; these are the striped regions. Where $u$ is locally
quadratic, near the maximum and in the tails, the pattern is a sampled
Fresnel zone plate. An undersampled zone plate replicates its centre:
neighbouring cells agree in colour wherever $q\,h\,\partial_mu$ is near an
even integer, so fake centres appear along the curves
$\{qh\,\partial_mu\in2\Z\}$, $m=1,2$. Figure~\ref{fig:res} shows the same
physical state at $64$ and $256$ cells with the same quantum: the island
lattice is present at $64$ and absent at $256$, where the frame is an
ordinary contour map. Table~\ref{tab:res} gives the measures. With the
quantum scaled as $1/N$ instead, the measures are constant in $N$
(perimeter per cell $0.879$, $0.894$, $0.893$): the texture depends on
the single ratio $qh|\nabla u|$ and is scale-free undersampling.

\section{What is not claimed, and what the lesson is}\label{sec:lesson}

The number of components of the pattern, and the number of local maxima
of $u$, are not monotone in general in two dimensions
\cite{Magnanini}; nor is the entropy of the value histogram of $u$. There
are no eddies: for $t>0$ the field is analytic with smooth nested
isotherms. The lesson of Part~I is methodological. Perimeter, block
entropy and component counts of a thresholded simulation measure the
sampling as much as the dynamics; the phase-averaging identity
\eqref{eq:phase} is a diagnostic, since a phase-averaged perimeter
exceeding $q\TV_h$ is aliasing by Theorem~\ref{thm:grid}(3). Real
complexity requires the nonlinearity inside the dynamics, which is the
subject of Part~II.

\part{Class-4 structures against the boundary strata}

\section{Moving the modulus into the dynamics}\label{sec:cca}

Wolfram's continuous cellular automata \cite[ch.~4, 6]{NKS} replace the
display rule of Part~I by a state rule:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cca}
  u^{l+1}_{\mathbf i}=\frc\Bigl(u^l_{\mathbf i}+\sum_{m=1}^nr_m\,\tilde\delta^2_{m,\tau_m(\mathbf i)}u^l_{\mathbf i}+a\Bigr),
\end{equation}
with $r_m=1/(2n+1)$ this is ``the mean of the $(2n+1)$-point
neighbourhood plus $a$, modulo $1$''. The operator
$\tilde\delta^2_{m,\tau_m}$ is the per-axis second difference of the
ternary-cube rule \cite{TernaryCube}: on a free digit it is the ordinary
central difference, on a wall digit it uses the reflected ghost value, so
the box has reflecting walls and the $3^n$ strata of the grid are exactly
the places where the rule differs. In one dimension Wolfram reports class-4
behaviour, with discrete localized structures on a periodic background,
for $a=0.398$, $a=0.4$, and for $a=0.5$ with the neighbours weighted by
$1.13$ before averaging \cite[p.~244]{NKS}.

\section{Questions}\label{sec:questions}

A localized structure that reaches a wall of codimension one meets a
cell whose neighbourhood is its own mirror image. In two dimensions it
can also meet an edge where two mirrors cross, and in three dimensions a
corner where three cross. We ask:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Do structures reflect, annihilate, or stick at a wall? Does the wall itself nucleate structures?
\item Is the time-averaged activity near a wall higher, lower or equal to the bulk value, and how does it depend on the codimension of the stratum (wall, edge, corner)?
\item Are there boundary-bound structures that exist only on a stratum, and does their class differ from the bulk class?
\end{enumerate}

\section{The reflection principle}\label{sec:reflection}

The first result is exact and settles the structure of the problem.
Write $\mathcal R_m$ for the even reflection of a configuration across
the wall nodes of axis $m$, which turns the box grid
$\{0,\dots,N_m-1\}$ into the periodic grid of length $2(N_m-1)$, and
$\mathcal R=\mathcal R_1\cdots\mathcal R_n$ for the reflection into the
torus $\mathbb T_h=\prod_m\Z/2(N_m-1)\Z$.

\begin{theorem}[reflection principle]\label{thm:reflection}
Let $F_{\rm box}$ be the rule \eqref{eq:cca} on the box with
ternary-cube mirror ghosts, and $F_{\rm per}$ the same rule on
$\mathbb T_h$ with periodic wrap. Then
\[
  \mathcal R\circ F_{\rm box}=F_{\rm per}\circ\mathcal R ,
\]
and $\mathcal R$ maps the box configurations bijectively onto the
reflection-symmetric configurations of $\mathbb T_h$, which form an
invariant set of $F_{\rm per}$. The same holds for any rule whose
per-axis stencil is symmetric under $i\mapsto-i$, in particular for the
weighted means of \cite[p.~244]{NKS}, and for any pointwise
post-processing such as $\frc$ or rounding.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At a node with free digit $\tau_m=1$ the two rules read the same
neighbours. At a wall node the mirror ghost $u_{-1}:=u_{1}$ is exactly
the value that the reflected configuration carries at the position
$-1$, so the stencil sums agree; symmetry of the stencil makes the
reflected configuration's update at $-i$ equal to its update at $i$, so
symmetry is preserved. Pointwise maps commute with everything.
\end{proof}

Numerically the identity holds to the last digit: on $400$ cells for
$3000$ steps in one dimension and on $64^2$ cells for $1000$ steps in two
dimensions, the maximum difference between the box run and the doubled
periodic run was $0$ (script \texttt{reflection\_principle.py}).

The consequences organize Part~II. A structure that hits a wall of
codimension one collides head-on with its own mirror image; what the
wall does to it is the outcome of a symmetric two-body collision. A
structure that reaches an edge of a three-dimensional box, or a corner of
a two-dimensional one, meets the images under two commuting reflections,
a symmetric four-body collision; a corner of a three-dimensional box is
an eight-body collision. The stratum of codimension $c$ is the fixed set
of $c$ commuting reflections, and the activity there is constrained
accordingly: the wall node is its own image, so the difference across
the wall is identically zero, and the boundary stratum is
\emph{quieter} than the bulk by construction, not by dynamics. Finally,
nothing can live on a wall that does not also exist in the periodic
system as a symmetric configuration, so boundary-bound structures are
exactly the mirror-symmetric localized structures of the bulk rule
pinned at the symmetry axis.

\section{One dimension}\label{sec:1d}

We take $a=0.398$, $r=\frac13$ (the three-cell mean), random initial
data, values rounded to nine decimals after each step to emulate exact
arithmetic, and the activity indicator
$\chi_{\mathbf i}=[\dist(u_{\mathbf i}-u_{\mathbf i+1},\Z)>0.05]$, which
is Wolfram's difference display thresholded. Figure~\ref{fig:class4_1d}
shows the space-time diagrams with periodic and with reflecting walls, and a magnified view of the wall.

The bulk activity fraction, averaged over four seeds and
$2500$ steps after a $500$-step transient, is $0.27$ with periodic
boundaries and $0.28$ with walls: intermediate between the extinct and
the saturated value, as class~4 requires. The profile against distance
to the wall is flat in the bulk and drops only in the wall layer: the
wall node has activity $0.09$ and its neighbour $0.23$, after which the
bulk value is reached. The ten cells nearest the walls carry $0.90$ of
the bulk activity. This is the quiet layer predicted by
Theorem~\ref{thm:reflection}: the wall node cannot differ from its own
image, and its neighbour sits in a symmetric three-cell neighbourhood.
No structure was seen to stick at the wall, and the wall did not
nucleate structures beyond those arriving from the bulk, consistent with
the collision picture: a head-on symmetric collision in this rule
returns the pair.

\section{Two and three dimensions}\label{sec:2d}

\paragraph{Scan.} In two dimensions the rule is the five-point mean plus
$a$ modulo $1$. A scan of $a$ in steps of $0.01$ on a $128^2$ torus from
random data (script \texttt{scan2d.py}) gives a late activity fraction
that varies \emph{continuously} with $a$, from $0.29$ near $a=0.01$ to
$0.92$ on the plateaus $a\in[0.34,0.43]$ and $[0.57,0.66]$, with dips to
$0.43$--$0.45$ near $a=\frac13,\frac12,\frac23$. There are no sharp
class-4 windows of the one-dimensional kind, and the one-dimensional
class-4 value $a=0.398$ is fully chaotic in two dimensions (activity
$0.92$, persistence of active cells equal to their density). Space-time
cuts (Figure~\ref{fig:cuts}) show three regimes: near $a=\frac13$ and
$\frac23$ the background is a period-three cycle and the dynamics
consists of slowly wandering \emph{domain walls} between its three
phases; near $a=\frac12$ one sees intermittent short-lived spots on a
period-two background; on the plateaus, spatiotemporal chaos. In this
family the two-dimensional analogue of the one-dimensional gliders is
therefore a codimension-one defect, the phase wall, and the natural
question becomes how phase walls and intermittent spots meet the box.

\paragraph{Activity per stratum.} We run the box with mirror walls,
classify every node by the codimension of its stratum in the ternary
cube ($0$ interior, $1$ wall, $2$ edge or two-dimensional corner, $3$

$128^2$ for $1500$ steps and $32^3$ for $800$ steps, averaged after a
transient of one third of the run (script \texttt{strata.py}).

Four observations. First, in the chaotic regime the activity decreases
monotonically with the codimension of the stratum, in both dimensions,
and the depth of the boundary layer is one cell: at distance one from
the nearest wall the two-dimensional activity is already $0.981$ against
$0.989$ in the bulk. This is the kinematic effect of
Theorem~\ref{thm:reflection}: a node on a stratum of codimension $c$ has
$c$ of its neighbour pairs identified by symmetry, so it has fewer
independent neighbours to differ from, and the effect compounds with
$c$. Second, in the intermittent regime the corner is strongly
suppressed in both dimensions ($0.19$ against $0.56$ in two dimensions,
$0.33$ against $0.48$ in three), while the three-dimensional faces and
edges are at the bulk value within the noise. So in two dimensions the
spots avoid the corners but not the walls, and in three dimensions only
the eight corner nodes are special; the twelve edges, which are the
analogue of the two-dimensional corners, are not. Third, the phase-wall
regime is the one in which the box is felt at a distance: the activity
profile against the distance to the nearest wall is
$0.35,\,0.40,\,0.44,\,0.47,\,0.48,\,0.49$ for distances $0$ to $5$,
a boundary layer about five cells deep, against one cell in the other
regimes. The reflection principle says why: a phase wall approaching a
mirror meets a phase wall of opposite orientation, its own image, and
the pair annihilates when the strip between them collapses, so a region
near the mirror is cleared of walls parallel to it; walls perpendicular
to the mirror end on it, and are the activity that remains at distance
zero. Fourth, the same $a=0.33$ is chaotic under the seven-point rule in
three dimensions, with the one-cell layer of the chaotic regime, which
shows that the regime, not the constant, is what the strata respond to.

\paragraph{What this does and does not show.} Within the mean-plus-constant
family, the strata of the ternary cube are visible as the reflection
principle predicts: a quiet layer whose strength grows with the
codimension, strongest at the corners, one cell deep where the bulk is
chaotic or intermittent and several cells deep where the bulk is
organized into phase domains, because there the mirror annihilates its
own image's walls. We did not find
two-dimensional class-4 structures of the glider type in this family, so
the question of how a glider reflects off an edge or a corner remains to
be asked of a rule that has gliders, for instance Lenia \cite{Lenia},
where the large convolution kernel makes the boundary strata
$(2s+1)^n$ layers thick in the sense of the ternary-cube note
\cite{TernaryCube}. The reflection principle applies there verbatim.
